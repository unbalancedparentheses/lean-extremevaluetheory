Extreme Value Theory in Lean

A machine-checked library of the mathematics of rare events: limits of maxima, heavy tails, the statistics of extremes, and extremes of dependent sequences, written in Lean 4 on top of Mathlib.

Read the blueprint API documentation Source on GitHub

Why you can trust it

Every proof is checked twice
Lean’s kernel checks each proof at build time, and every night the whole library is re-checked by con-leche, an independent kernel checker that itself comes with a machine-checked proof that it never accepts a proof of False.
No gaps, no extra axioms
About 80,000 lines with no sorry; the main theorems use only Lean’s three standard axioms.
Statements are compared with the literature
Six independent audits compared each formal statement with its textbook source. Every known difference is recorded in the faithfulness report.

What a theorem looks like

The Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem: if suitably normalized maxima of independent observations converge to a non-degenerate law, that law is Gumbel, Fréchet or Weibull, up to a change of location and scale.

theorem IsExtremeValueDistr.classification
    {G : CumulativeDistributionFunction}
    (hG : G.IsExtremeValueDistr) :
    (∃ A, A • G = standardGumbelCDF) ∨
      (∃ A ξ, ∃ (hξ : 0 < ξ), A • G = standardFrechetCDF hξ) ∨
      (∃ A ξ, ∃ (hξ : 0 < ξ), A • G = standardWeibullCDF hξ)

What is proved

Limits of maxima
Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko, max-stability, the GEV family and its moments, block maxima.
Domains of attraction
Fréchet, Weibull and Gumbel characterizations, von Mises conditions, the quantile form.
Peaks over threshold
Pickands–Balkema–de Haan as an equivalence for every shape.
Regular variation
Uniform convergence, representation, Potter bounds, Karamata, Breiman, extended and second-order variation, the multivariate theory and the converse Cramér–Wold device.
Stable laws
The generalized central limit theorem for every index, its converse, and the domains of attraction of stable and normal laws.
Heavy tails
Subexponential laws, random sums, ruin probabilities, linear processes.
Statistics
Hill, Pickands, moment and Weissman estimators; consistency, asymptotic normality, dependent data; GPD and GEV maximum likelihood.
Dependence
Leadbetter’s theorem, the extremal index, the tail process, mixing, Markov chains and moving averages.
Multivariate
Max-stable vectors and their spectral measures, the Pickands dependence function, tail dependence.
Processes
Poisson random measures, exceedance point processes, the Skorokhod space, the tail empirical process converging to Brownian motion.

The full catalogue lists every result with its Lean name.

Something new

Making every hypothesis explicit turned up a simple stationary sequence, the echo process, in which each large shock reappears once after a lag that grows with its size. Its tail process looks exactly like that of independent data, yet its extremal index is ½ rather than 1. As far as we can tell it is only the second known mechanism of this kind. Research notes.
